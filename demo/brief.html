Transaction Firewall

A safety checkpoint that lets an AI manage money without getting robbed.

The problem

AI assistants are starting to control real crypto wallets — and they can be tricked. Feed one a sneaky transaction, it signs it thinking it's routine, and the money is gone. The AI is both trusting and able to sign on its own, with no human to say "wait, this looks off."

Already happened

In May 2026, an attacker prompt-injected the Grok / Bankr AI agent (via a Morse-code message on X) into signing away 3 billion tokens — about $150,000 from the wallet it controlled.

What it is

A security guard that stands between the AI and the money. Before anything is signed, the guard inspects the transaction and can slam the door. If the verdict isn't safe, it refuses to sign — and without its signature, the funds physically cannot move. It's not a warning the AI can ignore; it's a locked door.

How the guard decides

Read What it really does Ignores the friendly label and finds the transaction's true intent — a "swap" that's actually "hand over my whole wallet."
Simulate Test-run in a sealed sandbox Runs the transaction inside a throwaway copy of the blockchain and watches the money. If it drains, the guard knows for certain — nothing real is touched.
Screen Check the wanted list Is the recipient a known thief? Uses a real, public list of ~2,500 criminal addresses, refreshed from the internet.
Rules Check your house rules No unlimited spending, no sending to strangers, nothing over your set limit.
